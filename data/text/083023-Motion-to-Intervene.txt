Finally, many of the outlet s who seek to intervene have significant experience 
broadcasting live courtroom proceedings, including in high profile trials that resulted in both 
acquittals ( Wisconsin v. Kyle Rittenhouse, Ohio v. Skyler Richardson ) and convictions ( South 
Carolina v. Alex Murdaugh ).  Their camera equipment and oper ators are experienced and not the 
large, clunky, numerous camera equipment and op erators that the Supreme Court expressed

camera operators in the courtroom for a pretrial hearing, and the equipment journalists used was 
noisy, required cables lying througho ut the courtroom, and, at trial, involved some newsreel-style 
cameras.  Id. at 536. The Court can take judicial noti ce that similar equipment is not involved 
here.  Plainly, the Suprem e Court’s concerns in Estes  do not require removal of the more modest, 
less intrusive camera equipment being used by reporters in this case. 
Accordingly, the media coalition seeks to intervene for the limited purpose of opposing 
Mr. Kohberger’s motion to remove  cameras from the courtroom a nd to make full argument and 
briefing on this issue.
