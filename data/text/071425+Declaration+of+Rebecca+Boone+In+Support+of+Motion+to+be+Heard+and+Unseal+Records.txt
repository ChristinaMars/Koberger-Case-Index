I, Rebecca Boone, declare and state as follows:  
1. I am the supervisory correspondent for Idaho for the Associated Press  (“AP”) . As 
such I have personal knowledge of the facts and statements contained in this declaration.  
2. I have 26 years of journalism industry experience, much of that time spent  covering 
the judicial branch and criminal justice issues.  
3. I have reported on the Nov. 13, 2022 deaths of four University of Idaho students, 
the subsequent criminal investigation and the arrest and prosecution of Bryan Kohberger.  
4. I graduated from the University of Idaho with a bachelor’s degree in journalism in 
1999, and I covered the courts and law enforcement beat for The Lewiston Tribune before I joined 
the AP  in 2002 as a reporter in the Boise bureau.  
5. In 2013 I was promoted to my current position of supervisory correspondent, 
tasked with leading AP’s news coverage in Idaho. I have extensive experience covering criminal and civil litigation in state and federal courts. I was an Idaho Press Club board mem ber from 2014 
until 2023, when I stepped down due to time constraints. I am a longtime advisory board member of the University of Idaho’s School of Journalism and Mass Media. I became a Loyola Law

6. High -profile criminal cases present unique challenges and unique opportunities. 
The Court has taken extensive and careful efforts to maintain Mr. Kohberger’s right to a fair trial. Now that Mr. Kohberger has reached a plea deal with the state, and has pleaded guilty, those fair trial concerns are resolved, and the Court should unseal court documents and increase transparency in this case.  
7. We understand that the Court must balance the Sixth Amendment rights of the 
accused with the First Amendment interests of the media, which serves as a proxy for the public. We do not seek extraordinary access now. Rather, it is our hope that the Court will  simply re -
weigh its balancing of Sixth Amendment and First Amendment rights now that Mr. Kohberger has pleaded guilty.  
I. Court records should be unsealed at the earliest opportunity . 
8. Idaho Court Administrative Rule 32 opens with a policy statement: “The public 
has a right to access the judicial department’s declarations of law and public policy, and to access the records of all proceedings open to the public.” That statement can serve as a guiding star when 
weighing whether court documents should remain sealed in the weeks ahead, particularly once a jury is seated and the trial is set to begin. While not every court document in this case is included

9. On June 26, I counted each order listed in the online case file, and then counted 
each order to seal. I did not include stipulations to seal or motions to seal in my assessment, nor 
did I include orders to redact rather than seal. Since this case began in 2022, there have been 465 orders issued by the court. More than half them – approximately 246 in all – are orders to seal. Six are orders to unseal.  
10. When the proceedings were being held in Latah County, the bulk of the sealing 
occurred at the beginning of the case and was mostly limited to material that had been returned from search warrants to internet and phone providers, banks , and the like.  
11. The majority of orders to seal have been issued since the case was moved to Ada 
County. It is perhaps worth noting that the number of sealed records in this case has skyrocketed in 2025. Of 168 total orders issued by this Court since Jan. 1, 2025, more tha n two -thirds of them 
– 103 in total – have been orders sealing documents from public view. Without being able to view 
the records, I can only speculate as to why that may be. Perhaps it is because the case was getting 
closer to trial. Perhaps it is because  of the amendments to Idaho Court Administrative Rule 32 
that took effect this year, lifting the requirement for a hearing before records may be sealed. 
Perhaps it is simply an anomaly. Regardless , there are more sealed records in this case than I can 
recall in any case I’ve covered for t he AP over the last 23 years.  
12. A substantial portion of the evidence, arguments and rulings in this case have been 
delivered in the form of written documents rather than in open court. The documents are as much

13. Many of the sealed documents have been kept secret in order to ensure that a fair 
and impartial jury may be seated, so that the defendant can receive his constitutional right to a fair trial. Now that a jury will not be impaneled, the C ourt should unseal the documents and honor 
Idaho’s constitutional promise of open courts.  
II. Court practices set informal precedent, and a culture of secrecy should not be the 
norm for Idaho.  
14. In court systems as small as Idaho’s, practices in one courtroom can easily set the 
culture for other courtrooms. That’s why it is crucial that transparency and access are  scrupulously 
maintained in every case, but especially in high- profile cases.  
15. There is no longer any risk of tainting a jury pool in this case. Mr. Kohberger has 
reached a plea deal with the state and has pleaded  guilty in exchange for consecutive life 
sentences . He has waived  his right to a fair trial. I respectfully urge the Court to re -weigh the 
balance of the First Amendment right of the press and the public against the concerns that led to 
the increased secrecy in this case, and suggest that such a re- weighing will likely lead to unsealed 
court documents.
